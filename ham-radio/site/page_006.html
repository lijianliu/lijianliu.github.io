💡 Space = above 50 km. Repeaters re-send signals on another channel. Coordinators are chosen by local hams.

📡 What is an auxiliary station?

An auxiliary station is a helper station. It doesn't talk to people. It sends signals from one point to another inside a group of ham stations that work together, usually to help run a repeater.

Example: a repeater with a remote receiver. A repeater sits on a hill. To hear weak handheld radios in a valley far away, the club puts an extra receiver in that valley. That receiver needs a way to send what it hears back to the repeater, so a small transmitter beams it one way to the main repeater on a separate channel. That one-way link is an auxiliary station (the "one-way link" panel in the picture above).

  • ✔ Auxiliary station: one-way transmissions between a remote repeater receiver and the main repeater transmitter.
  • ✘ Not one: a backup radio kept for emergencies. That's just a spare radio.
  • ✘ Not one: a station in the Military Auxiliary Radio System (MARS). The word "auxiliary" is a trap!
  • ✘ Not one: a second station at a multi-operator contest site.

Who picks their channels? Repeater and auxiliary stations need channels that won't bump into each other. A Volunteer Frequency Coordinator recognized by local amateurs recommends them. The coordinator is chosen by local or regional hams whose stations could be repeater or auxiliary stations, not by the FCC.
